§01 What Is Stellaris?

The stars had been there long before anyone thought to reach for them. They were there before the first carbon molecule folded into something that called itself alive, before the first nervous system fired the first thought that might have been curiosity, before the first hand-like appendage pointed upward at a burning point in the dark and made a sound that meant: what is that, and can we go there? The stars did not care. They continued burning, as they always had. It was the reaching that changed everything.

// OPENING NARRATION FRAGMENT · GALACTIC HISTORICAL ARCHIVE · CIRCA 2200 //

Stellaris is a real-time grand strategy game with pausable simulation, developed by Paradox Development Studio and published by Paradox Interactive. It is set in the year 2200 and casts the player as the leader of one of hundreds of possible spacefaring civilisations taking their first steps into the galaxy. What follows — across decades or centuries of in-game time — is a story of exploration, colonisation, diplomacy, war, scientific discovery, cultural evolution, and, inevitably, contact with forces older and stranger than your young civilisation can comfortably process.

Unlike most strategy games, Stellaris does not have a fixed scenario. Every game begins with a procedurally generated galaxy populated by dozens of AI-controlled empires, dozens more pre-sapient and primitive civilisations, and scattered throughout: the remains of something that came before you and left warnings you probably shouldn't ignore. No two games are the same, and the game's richness comes not from a designed narrative but from the emergent stories that arise when its interlocking systems interact under the pressure of player choices.

What Stellaris Is Not

It is not a real-time strategy game — you cannot control individual ships in battle in the way a StarCraft player would. Battles resolve automatically when two fleets meet; your influence comes through preparation, design, and positioning, not through reaction. It is not a pure 4X game in the Civilisation tradition — the diplomatic and political systems are substantially more complex than a conventional 4X, and the mid-to-late game introduces narrative crises and galaxy-scale events that would be alien to most 4X players. It is closer to Crusader Kings or Europa Universalis in a space setting than it is to Civilisation or Master of Orion. Understanding this distinction prevents the most common new-player frustration: expecting to control more than the game allows.

§02 Game Overview & Versions

Stellaris has been continuously developed since its initial release in May 2016. The current version as of this guide's writing is in the 4.x series, which introduced one of the most significant mechanical overhauls in the game's history: the transition from individual Pop management to Pop Group mechanics, a complete rework of how Districts and Buildings interact with workforce, and major rebalancing of the economic systems that had accumulated power creep over years of DLC.

Everything else adds depth to specific playstyles rather than fundamental mechanics. Do not try to learn all DLC mechanics simultaneously on your first playthrough.

§03 The Galaxy & Map

The survey ship catalogued the system in four hours: two gas giants, one asteroid belt, one habitable world with an oxygen-nitrogen atmosphere and surface temperatures in the acceptable range. The crew filed the standard reports. The captain sent the standard signal: system claimed, colonisation assessment pending. Nobody mentioned that the planet's continents were arranged in the shape of a screaming face. It had probably been that way forever. It was probably nothing.

// SURVEY LOG 7742-BETA · YEAR 2207 · EXECUTIVE OUTCOMES INTERSTELLAR //

The Stellaris galaxy is a star map containing between 200 and 1,000 systems (depending on game settings), connected by hyperlane networks. Each system contains one star, a variable number of planets and moons, and various spatial objects (asteroid belts, nebulae, anomalies, derelict structures). Understanding the galaxy's structure is prerequisite to every strategic decision.

Galaxy Shapes

ShapeCharacteristicsStrategic Implications
Spiral (2/3/4/5 arms)Arms of densely connected systems radiating from a central core; the core connects all armsChokepoints in arm narrows; galactic core as late-game objective; most common recommended shape for new players
EllipticalDense and uniformly distributed; many connections; few natural chokepointsHighly connected; fast expansion; difficult to defend; favours aggressive military expansion strategies
RingSystems arranged in a circle; only connects along the ringNatural chokepoints at key junctions; linear expansion; fewer neighbours at any given point
IrregularRandom distribution with variable densityUnpredictable; can create natural isolated zones or hyper-connected regions; high variance between games

Hyperlanes and Chokepoints

All FTL travel (in the default setting since 2.0) uses the Hyperlane network. You can only travel along established hyperlane connections. This makes the galaxy's topology critically important: a system with only one hyperlane connection is a natural chokepoint that can be defended from a single starbase. Identifying these on your galaxy map and building Gateway Starbases on them is one of the most impactful early-to-mid-game strategic decisions.

Nebulae

Nebulae are special regions of space that affect sensor range (reducing it) and certain technology draw weights. Empires inside nebulae receive a natural intelligence advantage — enemies have reduced sensor range in the cloud. Some technologies and traits interact specifically with nebulae. Note when setting up a game whether your home system starts inside or adjacent to a nebula.

§04 Species & Traits

The species questionnaire was twelve pages long. It asked about metabolic rate, reproductive strategy, social cohesion index, cognitive flexibility, attitude toward hierarchy, thermal comfort range, and preferred linguistic register. It did not ask whether the species was kind. Kindness, the designers noted in internal memos, was not a trait that could be meaningfully parameterised. It emerged from combinations of other things, or it did not. That was, arguably, the whole problem.

// GALACTIC SEED ALGORITHM DOCUMENTATION · PARADOX INTERNAL ARCHIVE //

Your species is the foundation of your empire. At empire creation, you design your founder species by selecting a portrait (appearance), naming it, and allocating trait points to purchase species traits. Traits provide permanent statistical modifiers that apply to every Pop of that species throughout the game. Every trait has a point cost (positive traits cost points; negative traits grant refunded points); your starting budget is 2 points, which can be expanded by taking negative traits.

Trait Categories

Traits affect five broad categories of Pop performance:

  • Resource output: Traits that modify how much a Pop produces from their job. Industrious (+15% Minerals), Intelligent (+10% Research from research jobs), Agrarian (+15% Food) are the foundational examples.
  • Pop growth and assembly: Rapid Breeders (+10% Pop Growth Speed), Slow Breeders (-10%), Budding (a unique mechanic for some fungoid species allowing asexual reproduction).
  • Habitability: Every planet has a planetary class with an associated climate preference (Continental, Ocean, Arid, etc.). Traits that match your preference range expand which worlds you can colonise efficiently. Tomb World Preference, for example, allows an empire to colonise irradiated nuclear hellscapes most species avoid.
  • Amenities and happiness: Traits affecting how many Amenities pops consume or how difficult they are to keep happy. Charismatic (+20% Amenities from jobs), Communal (-10% Housing), Decadent (+20% Consumer Goods upkeep on slaves).
  • Military: Strong (+5% Army Damage, +20% Leader skill level for Generals), Very Strong (+10%/+40%), Weak (negative).

Recommended Trait Combinations

Research Focus
RECOMMENDED FOR: TECH RUSH BUILDS

Intelligent (+2 points) is mandatory. Add Natural Engineers, Natural Physicists, or Natural Sociologists for additional research output in specific categories. Compensate the high point cost by taking Repugnant (-20% Amenities from jobs) — offset with Entertainer jobs. The research advantage compounds over the entire game.

Production Focus
RECOMMENDED FOR: ECONOMIC BUILDS

Industrious or Agrarian depending on economy type. Combine with Rapid Breeders for faster Pop growth (more workers faster). Weak (-0.5 points) is an acceptable negative trait for production builds since army damage matters less than economy.

Adaptive Colonisers
RECOMMENDED FOR: WIDE EXPANSION

Adaptive (+2 points; +10% habitability) or Extremely Adaptive (+4 points; +20% habitability). Pays for itself on large galaxies by enabling efficient colonisation of more world types. Combine with Rapid Breeders. Expensive point-wise but enables territory control strategies other builds cannot.

Lithoid Builds
SPECIES TYPE: LITHOID ONLY

Lithoid species eat Minerals instead of Food and have innate habitability advantages. The Lithoid origin (requires the trait) starts you on a world other species would find barely habitable. Extremely effective for mining-focused builds. No food upkeep makes the economy significantly simpler.

§05 Ethics & Authority

The philosophers had been arguing for three centuries about whether a truly egalitarian society could survive contact with the galaxy's less egalitarian inhabitants without eventually becoming one of them. The evidence from the historical record was not encouraging. The counter-argument, which the philosophers who survived long enough to make it generally agreed was compelling, was that the alternative — becoming what you feared before anyone else forced you to — was worse, and faster, and harder to come back from.

// ACADEMIC PROCEEDINGS · INSTITUTE OF POLITICAL THEORY · YEAR 2280 //

Ethics define your empire's fundamental political and philosophical position. They determine which Civics you can select, how your pops react to your policies, which diplomatic actions are available or prohibited, and how other empires perceive and interact with you. Your empire starts with 3 ethics points to spend. Regular Ethics cost 1 point each; Fanatic Ethics cost 2 points and provide doubled bonuses.

Ethics and Drift

Your empire's ethics are not fixed after game start. Over time, Factions gain influence and push your empire's ethics toward their preferred values. An empire that starts Egalitarian but consistently suppresses factions and exploits labour will find its ethics drifting Authoritarian. Conversely, an Authoritarian empire that grants broad rights and builds prosperity will drift Egalitarian. Managing ethical drift — either allowing it deliberately or fighting it through edicts and policies — is an ongoing mid-game governance task.

§06 Civics

Civics are the specific institutions and principles that define how your empire functions in practice. They are the most granular layer of empire customisation at game start, providing specific mechanical bonuses and unlocking unique buildings, units, and interactions. Empires start with two civics; a third is unlocked by researching Galactic Administration technology. Civics can be reformed every 20 years (spending 10 Unity per point of Empire Size).

Highly Effective Civics

Technocracy
MATERIALIST · RESEARCH PRODUCTION

Researchers produce +1 Unity; +10% Research output. Excellent in combination with research-focused traditions. One of the most consistently powerful production-boosting civics. Research compounds throughout the game; this bonus pays dividends at every stage.

Meritocracy
OLIGARCHIC · LEADER BONUSES

+1 Skill Level to Leaders; +3 Ruler Edict Capacity. Leaders are integral to the 4.x system. A Scientist with higher skill levels generates more research; an Admiral with higher skill wins more battles. This civic's bonus scales with the entire leader system and never becomes irrelevant.

Mining Guilds
MATERIALIST-COMPATIBLE · MINERALS

Miners produce more Minerals; Mineral districts more effective. Minerals are the foundation of every economy — alloys, consumer goods, buildings all cost minerals. A Mineral surplus in the early game enables faster expansion, faster ship building, faster everything. One of the best foundation civics for wide expansion.

Anglers
OCEAN WORLD · HYBRID PRODUCTION

Agriculture districts produce Anglers and Pearl Divers, outputting Food, Consumer Goods, and Trade Value simultaneously. One of the strongest economy-diversification civics in 4.x — transforms previously weak Agricultural districts into multi-output powerhouses. Best on Ocean worlds.

Barbaric Despoilers
MILITARIST/XENOPHOBE · RAIDING

Raiding orbital bombardment stance kidnaps Pops from enemy worlds. Special Despoil war goal. Allows Pop importation without colonisation overhead. One of the most aggressive expansion-without-colonisation builds in the game. Synergises with Warrior Caste civ and Militarist ethics.

Inward Perfection
PACIFIST · TALL/INTERNAL FOCUS

Cannot establish Branch Offices; cannot join Federations; cannot claim systems; +20% all resource output from Jobs; +10% Unity. The tall-play civic: sacrifices all aggressive options for enormous domestic production bonuses. Extremely powerful on small galaxies or when expecting a long peaceful middle game. Cannot make claims but can still go to war to defend territory.

Civic Synergy: The Combo Is What Matters

Individual civics rarely define a build. Combinations do. Technocracy + Distinguished Admiralty (more admiral skill levels, more naval cap) creates a military-technological powerhouse. Meritocracy + Philosopher King (ruler is a Scientist) creates a research engine with leader bonuses. Inward Perfection + High populations create production empires that can outtech everyone. Before finalising civics, think about which traditions and ascension perks they synergise with.

Prosperous Unification is not the most exciting origin but it is consistently the most reliable. While you learn the game's systems, PU prevents the "I ran out of minerals in year 2210" problem that kills many first playthroughs of more ambitious origins.

§08 Gestalt Empires

The question the diplomats from organic empires always asked, eventually, was: does the Hive know that it's a Hive? The answer, the Hive generally replied — insofar as it replied at all — was that the question presupposed a distinction between the knower and the known that the Hive had no framework to process. The diplomats found this unsatisfying. The Hive found the diplomats confusing. Both parties, in the diplomatic log, were recorded as finding the encounter broadly non-violent, which was considered a success.

// GALACTIC DIPLOMATIC RECORD · FIRST CONTACT ARCHIVE · YEAR 2241 //

Gestalt Consciousness empires — Hive Minds and Machine Intelligences — represent a completely different playstyle from standard empires. They are not empires in the conventional political sense; they are unified organisms or collective intelligences with a single will expressed through all pops simultaneously.

Hive Mind

Hive Minds require the Gestalt Consciousness ethic (which takes all 3 ethics points). They cannot have Factions, cannot experience unhappiness in the conventional sense, use Drone jobs instead of standard Pop jobs, and consume Food instead of Consumer Goods. Key mechanical differences:

  • No Happiness or Amenities system — replace with Hive Coordination and Drone Upkeep
  • No Immigration or Emigration — the Hive does not permit this
  • Unique Devouring Swarm civic: most extreme aggressive variant; consumes everything; no diplomacy possible
  • Terravore civic: can eat entire planets for resources
  • Unique Psionic ascension path: the Hive Transcendence leading to Psionic Hive
  • Cannot integrate other species without Assimilation civic; will exterminate otherwise

Machine Intelligence

Machine Intelligences are robotic collectives requiring Machine Intelligence authority. They have no Food upkeep — machines don't eat — and instead assemble new Pops (at Energy cost) rather than growing them. Key differences:

  • Pop Assembly instead of Pop Growth; controllable production rate
  • No Consumer Goods upkeep; significant economic advantage in mid-game
  • Unique Maintenance Drones and Civilian Drone jobs
  • Unique Synthetic Evolution ascension path and unique crisis capability with Nemesis DLC
  • Driven Assimilator: converts organic species into machine pops; unique interaction with biological worlds
  • Rogue Servitor: protects organic species as pets/preserved beings; unique economic structure
Gestalt Empires Are Not Beginner Friendly

Hive Minds and Machine Intelligences are powerful but their mechanical uniqueness makes them poor learning environments. Understand the standard empire's Pop, faction, happiness, and consumer goods systems first. Then try a Machine Intelligence, because the absence of consumer goods and the Pop Assembly system makes Machine empires mechanically simpler than Hive Minds once you understand what they're doing. Devouring Swarm is the most extreme and should only be attempted by experienced players who want a specific aggressive challenge.

The Consumer Goods Bottleneck

The most common economic crisis for new players in Stellaris is Consumer Goods deficit. Every non-slave, non-gestalt Pop consumes Consumer Goods as part of their upkeep. As your population grows, your Consumer Goods consumption grows proportionally. Consumer Goods are produced by Artisan jobs, which require Industrial Districts. If your Alloy production uses most of your Industrial Districts, Consumer Goods production is squeezed. Watch your Consumer Goods balance carefully in the mid-game. A deficit causes Happiness collapse, which causes faction unrest, which destabilises your entire economy. Always maintain a buffer surplus.

§10 Pops & Pop Groups (4.x System)

In version 4.0, Stellaris transitioned from individual Pop management to a Pop Group system. Instead of tracking hundreds of individual pops, the game groups pops by species, strata, and job type into cohesive Pop Groups that produce resources collectively based on their Workforce contribution.

How Pop Groups Work

Each Pop Group represents a collection of pops sharing the same species, social stratum (Ruler, Specialist, Worker, Slave, Drone), and job. They contribute Workforce to jobs rather than being individually assigned. Districts provide jobs; buildings provide jobs; Workforce from Pop Groups fills those jobs; and output is calculated based on how many Workforce units are filling each job slot.

Pop Growth and Assembly

Standard empires grow Pops biologically. Growth rate is affected by:

  • Traits (Rapid Breeders, Slow Breeders)
  • Planet Stability and Happiness (higher = faster growth)
  • Housing available (insufficient housing severely reduces growth)
  • Amenities (insufficient Amenities reduce Happiness and thus growth)
  • Cloning Vats (if researched and built, provide additional assembly)

Pop growth is now empire-wide and distributed across all colonies rather than being generated independently on each planet. This means your total empire pop growth rate determines how many new pops are added across all colonies, and planets with good conditions will attract more of that growth. Maximising your empire-wide growth rate requires addressing it systematically rather than planet by planet.

§11 Planets: Districts & Buildings

The colony ship arrived containing 20,000 colonists, six prefabricated administrative modules, a complete geological survey, 400 automated mining drones, and a strongly worded letter from the Colonial Development Committee outlining the minimum acceptable output targets for years one through fifteen. The colonists reviewed the targets, reviewed the planet, reviewed the targets again, and began building.

// COLONIAL SETTLEMENT RECORD · YEAR 2215 · FRINGE SYSTEMS AUTHORITY //

In Stellaris 4.x, the planet economy works through an integrated District-Building-Job system. Understanding how these three layers interact is the foundation of effective planetary management.

Districts

Districts are the basic structural slots on a planet. Each planet has a maximum district count based on its planet size. Districts come in several types and each provides base jobs:

  • City Districts: Provide Housing and Clerk jobs; also generate one Building Slot per City District above the first. Building Slots are the key constraint on advanced production buildings.
  • Industrial Districts: Provide Artisan (Consumer Goods) and Foundry (Alloys) jobs; managed via District Specialisation.
  • Agricultural Districts: Provide Farmer jobs (Food); can be enhanced by Anglers civic.
  • Mining Districts: Provide Miner jobs (Minerals).
  • Generator Districts: Provide Technician jobs (Energy Credits).

District Specialisation (4.x)

A major 4.x feature is District Specialisation: Industrial Districts can be specialised toward either Consumer Goods or Alloys production, providing additional jobs of the chosen type per district. This replaces the old system where both Artisans and Foundry workers competed for the same district slots. Specialise your industrial districts to match your planet's role in your economy.

Buildings

Buildings are placed in Building Slots (which City Districts provide). They are more expensive than Districts but provide higher-quality specialist jobs. Key principles:

  • Research Labs: Provide Researcher jobs; upgrade for more and better-quality research. Your most important early-game buildings on core worlds.
  • Administrative Offices: Provide Bureaucrat jobs that reduce Empire Sprawl penalty. Essential as your empire expands.
  • Holo-Theatres / Entertainment Forums: Provide Entertainer jobs that generate Amenities. Required to prevent Amenities deficit as population grows.
  • Alloy Foundries: Provide additional Foundry jobs; essential on dedicated forge worlds.
  • Capital Building: Every world has a capital building slot; the planet's tier of capital provides ruler jobs and administrative functions. Upgrade your Homeworld capital priority.
Building Priority Rule

In the first 20 years: Capital upgrades on your homeworld, then Research Labs, then Administrative buildings once Empire Sprawl becomes relevant. In the mid-game: Alloy Foundries on dedicated Forge Worlds; Consumer Goods buildings on dedicated industrial worlds; Research Labs on your science worlds. Buildings are expensive — invest in the buildings that compound most over time, which means Research Labs for tech advancement and Alloy Foundries for military sustainability.

§12 Planet Specialisation

Once you have multiple colonies, the most effective approach to planetary management is specialisation: designating each world to a specific economic function rather than trying to make every planet self-sufficient in every resource. This creates a division of labour across your empire that maximises output from each world's particular strengths.

Common Planet Designations

Research World
PHYSICS / SOCIETY / ENGINEERING OUTPUT

Maximise City Districts (for Building Slots), Research Labs in all Building Slots, and prioritise Population growth to fill Researcher jobs. Feed this world from your agricultural worlds. This planet does not produce Minerals, Energy, or Alloys — it produces the technologies that multiply everyone else's output. Your homeworld is often your first Research World.

Forge World
ALLOY PRODUCTION

Maximum Industrial Districts specialised toward Foundries; Alloy Foundry buildings in all available slots. This world builds your fleet. Do not compromise its Alloy output for Consumer Goods; have a separate Consumer Goods world. The Forge World Planetary Designation provides +10% Foundry Job Output.

Agricultural World
FOOD PRODUCTION

Maximum Agricultural Districts; Feed all colonies from this world. Agriculture can be combined with Consumer Goods production if using the Anglers civic. Agricultural World designation provides +10% Farmer Job Output. One or two agricultural worlds typically sustains a mid-sized empire's Food requirements.

Mining World / Ecumenopolis
MINERALS / MAXIMUM PRODUCTION

Mining Districts on worlds with high Mineral deposits; Ecumenopolis ascension transforms a city world into the most productive planet type in the game (massive Industrial output). Ecumenopolis requires the Ascension Perk and is a late-game transformation; treat it as an endgame Alloy engine.

§13 Sectors & Governors

As your empire expands beyond your ability to micromanage every planet, Sectors become the administrative layer that organises your colonies into manageable groups. Each Sector can have a Sector Governor — a Leader whose skills and traits provide bonuses to all planets within that sector.

Governor Skills and the Leader System (4.x)

With Galactic Paragons DLC, Leaders gain traits at every level and have unique background histories that affect their capabilities. A Governor with high Skill Level provides:

  • Production bonuses to their sector's planets
  • Stability contributions to their planets
  • Specific bonus effects from their individual traits

Governor trait synergies are a significant quality-of-life multiplier: a Governor with the Architectural Enthusiasm trait (buildings cheaper) assigned to your primary construction sector reduces building costs across every world in that sector simultaneously.

Martial Law and Military Governors

In 4.3, setting a Commander as a planet or sector Governor applies Martial Law: they provide a direct Stability bonus to any planet they are controlling, not just their home planet. On newly conquered worlds with unhappy alien pops, a Military Governor with high Skill Level can stabilise the planet faster than purely administrative approaches.

§14 Trade & Markets

The Galactic Market (requires Federations DLC or sufficient game age) allows empires to buy and sell basic resources. The Market provides an economic safety valve: a temporary deficit in one resource can be covered by selling a surplus of another. Key market principles:

  • Joining the Galactic Market requires gaining access (through being founded or a specific event); the founding member has a permanent Price advantage on all trades.
  • Market prices fluctuate based on supply and demand from all participating empires. If everyone is selling Minerals, Mineral prices drop. Anticipate market movements rather than reacting to them.
  • Trade Value (generated by Clerk jobs and Merchant positions) converts to Energy Credits based on trade routes and policies. Trade-focused civics and ethics (Xenophile, Pacifist, Corporate) generate significantly more value from trade routes than others.
  • Strategic resource trading is often more valuable than selling basic resources — negotiate a trade for Exotic Gases or Rare Crystals that you lack rather than buying from the market, where prices can be steep.
The MegaCorp Economic Engine

Corporate authority empires (MegaCorp DLC) generate their primary income through Branch Offices: commercial buildings established on other empires' worlds that produce Trade Value for the Corporation. A well-developed Corporate empire with multiple Branch Offices across several neighbouring empires generates enormous Trade Value income without any military cost. The Corporate playstyle is an economic domination strategy that wins through economic supremacy rather than military conquest. Highly recommended for players who want a complex but non-violent path to galactic dominance.

§15 Technology

The technologies available in your draw depend on what you've already researched, what your Scientists' specialisations are, and various other factors.

§18 Ascension Paths

Three ways to transcend the biological lottery. The Materialists said: augment the body and the mind with machines until the distinction between organic and synthetic is a matter of preference rather than physiology. The Spiritualists said: augment the mind with something that has nothing to do with machines, that reaches for the space between stars rather than the material of which stars are made. And the Biologists said: stop augmenting and start redesigning, because the answer was always in the code and the code could be rewritten. All three schools had their adherents. All three schools occasionally produced someone who was unsettling in ways their particular framework could not fully explain.

Ascension Paths are end-game civilisation-defining choices accessed through Ascension Perks. Each path fundamentally transforms how your pops work and what capabilities your empire has. MATERIALIST · REQUIRES: UTOPIA DLC

Transforms your organic pops into synthetic beings. Final state: pops become Robots with massive efficiency bonuses; no food; no happiness concerns; extreme late-game productivity. The path's full benefits require completing multiple technologies that take years to research. Plan your Tradition completion order to unlock enough Ascension Perk slots for both your early utility perks (Executive Vigor, Technological Ascendancy) and your chosen path by mid-game.

§19 Exploration & Survey

The early game of Stellaris is almost entirely an exploration and survey race. Your Science Ships, commanded by Scientist leaders, scan every system in your immediate neighbourhood for planets, resources, anomalies, and strategically significant features. Speed of survey directly determines how quickly you can plan expansion, identify threats, and discover the galaxy's early secrets.

Science Ship Functions

  • Survey missions: Systematically scan every celestial body in a system to reveal resources, planet habitability values, and anomaly locations. The primary early-game task.
  • Anomaly investigation: When a survey uncovers an anomaly, the Science Ship can investigate it. Anomalies trigger events that provide resources, technologies, story content, or threats. Higher-level Scientist leaders reduce investigation failure chance.
  • Research Assistance: Science ships can assist planets with Research bonuses when not surveying. Less valuable than surveying in the early game but useful mid-game gap-filling.

Early Exploration Priorities

Year 2200–2210: Immediate Neighbours

Deploy all Science Ships (start with two if possible) to survey your immediate home system and the three or four systems connected to it by hyperlane. Identify habitable worlds for early colonisation, strategic resource deposits for mining stations, and blockers in your expansion path. The systems within 5 hyperlane jumps of your home system are the most important survey targets in the game.

Year 2210–2230: Border Contact and Expansion Path

Extend survey coverage to your borders with other empires. Understanding your neighbours' territorial extent early allows you to make Influence investment decisions before borders close around unclaimed systems. Every unclaimed habitable system that another empire colonises instead of you is a permanent strategic disadvantage that compounds over decades.

§20 Colonisation

Colonisation is how you transform surveyed worlds into productive members of your empire. The core decision is not which worlds to colonise but when and in what order, given the significant upfront investment each colony requires before it becomes economically productive.

Colonisation Economics

A new colony is an economic drain for several years after founding. The colony ship itself is expensive; the colony starts with only a few Pops and limited infrastructure; and maintaining it costs resources before its production scales up. The key rule: never found a colony you cannot sustain. A new colony that causes Consumer Goods or Energy deficits across your empire does more damage than it provides benefit in the short term.

Habitability and Its Importance

Every species has a planetary preference type. Pops working on worlds with low habitability produce less output and have lower happiness. In 4.0, habitability now affects job efficiency directly: a Pop on a planet at 70% habitability is less efficient than the same Pop at 100% habitability. Prioritise colonising worlds with 70%+ habitability for your founder species, or use Biological Ascension to create sub-species adapted to specific world types.

Wide vs. Tall

The classic Stellaris strategic tension: colonise widely (many planets, more total output, higher Empire Sprawl) or focus on developing fewer planets deeply (less Sprawl, more efficiency per planet, less administrative overhead). In 4.x, the penalty for excessive Empire Sprawl has been rebalanced to be less punishing than in earlier versions, shifting the meta slightly toward medium-wide builds rather than pure tall. However, true tall play (fewer, heavily developed worlds with high-level buildings and maxed districts) remains viable and is simpler to manage in the mid-game.

§21 Empire Sprawl & Influence

Empire Sprawl is a measure of administrative complexity. As your empire grows — more systems, more colonies, more planets — your Sprawl increases and begins generating a penalty to research speed, tradition adoption cost, and other outputs. Managing Sprawl through Administrative buildings (Bureaucrats who reduce the penalty) is a continuous mid-to-late game task.

Sprawl Reduction

  • Bureaucrat jobs from Administrative Office buildings directly reduce Empire Sprawl. Every major world should eventually have Administrative Offices to offset their own Sprawl contribution.
  • Sector Governors with administrative traits reduce Sprawl in their sector.
  • Domination traditions reduce the Sprawl generated by each colony.
  • Releasing territory as Vassals removes their Sprawl from your total while potentially keeping their resources accessible through Tribute arrangements.

Influence

Influence is the resource of political and territorial power. It is generated slowly (typically 3–5 per month) and spent on everything territorial:

  • Claiming systems before or during war (systems must be claimed to be permanently annexed)
  • Building Starbases (each Starbase beyond your limit costs Influence maintenance)
  • Activating Edicts
  • Faction appeasing
  • Diplomatic actions
  • Subject interactions

Influence is almost always the binding constraint on early-game expansion. You will frequently have the minerals and energy to colonise but not the Influence to claim the systems. Maximising Influence income through Rulers, the Beacon of Liberty achievement (Egalitarian), Rivals, and specific traditions is one of the highest-value strategic actions in the early-to-mid game.

§23 Fleets & Naval Capacity

The doctrine was simple: never engage with fewer ships than you have. The obvious flaw was that the enemy had read the same doctrine and was applying it simultaneously. Both fleets spent three months manoeuvring around each other in the Kerenthis nebula, each fleet commander certain that the other commander would make the first mistake. Eventually one of them ran low on fuel. The battle that followed lasted eleven minutes. It was, by the standards of the war, decisive.

// FLEET OPERATIONS ARCHIVE · FIRST KERENTHIS ENGAGEMENT · YEAR 2289 //

Naval Capacity is the ceiling on how many ships you can maintain without paying the Upkeep penalty for over-capacity fleets. Building beyond your Naval Capacity is possible but expensive — ships over-cap cost double Energy Credits per ship in upkeep. Managing the tension between military readiness and economic cost is the core of Stellaris military management.

Naval Capacity Sources

  • Technology: Multiple technologies increase base Naval Capacity (currently up to 20 repeatable increments in 4.3). Prioritising these is important for military-focused builds.
  • Starbases: Each Starbase tier and its Anchorage modules add Naval Capacity.
  • Traditions (Supremacy tree): Multiple bonuses to Naval Capacity from the Supremacy tradition.
  • Civics: Distinguished Admiralty adds +20 Naval Capacity and Admiral skill bonuses.
  • Edicts: Activation Protocols edict adds temporary Naval Capacity at Unity cost.

Fleet Composition Principles

Effective fleet composition in 4.x requires understanding the weapon-vs-defense matchup system:

  • Shields vs. Energy Weapons: Energy weapons (lasers, disruptors) are strong against shields but weaker against armor. Shields are ineffective against sustained energy fire.
  • Armor vs. Kinetic Weapons: Kinetic weapons (railguns, artillery) penetrate armor more effectively but are weaker against shields.
  • Disruptors: In 4.3, start with 50% shield and armor bypass at tier 1, increasing to 80% at Psionic tier. Highly effective but lower raw damage.
  • Missile ships: Ignores point-defense except for specialized PD ships. Strong but require a counter-PD investment.
  • Design your fleet to counter what your enemies are actually fielding, not to be theoretically optimal against an imaginary enemy.

§26 Armies & Planetary Warfare

Conquering a planet requires two steps: defeating the orbital fleet and bombarding the planet to softness, then landing armies to physically occupy it. Armies are a frequently neglected system that new players discover matters enormously when they've defeated every enemy fleet and cannot actually take the planets.

Army Types

  • Assault Armies: Standard offensive troops; recruited from planets; require time and minerals to produce.
  • Robotic Armies: Stronger than Assault Armies; available to robot-producing empires.
  • Clone Armies: Available to Clone Army origin; the strongest standard armies in the game early on.
  • Psionic Armies: Powerful armies available through Psionic Ascension; Strong trait pops improve Soldier efficiency in 4.3.
  • Battle Thralls: Slave armies; good cost-effectiveness; receive Soldier efficiency bonuses in 4.3.

Orbital Bombardment

Before landing armies, you can bombard the planet from orbit. This reduces the defending army's strength and the planet's Fortification level (which adds to defender strength). Bombardment stances range from Selective (avoids civilian casualties but slower) to Armageddon (destroys pops permanently; extreme crime against most ethics). Match your bombardment stance to your diplomatic situation — Armageddon bombardment will cause massive opinion penalties with most of the galaxy.

§29 The Galactic Community

The Galactic Community forms when enough empires have established communications — typically by year 2230–2250 in a standard game. It is a galaxy-wide legislative body where resolutions are proposed, debated, and voted upon, affecting all member empires with galaxy-wide policies.

How the Galactic Community Works

Member empires have Diplomatic Weight that determines their voting power. Diplomatic Weight is generated by your economy, technology, military power, and specific bonuses from traditions and civics. The empire with the most Diplomatic Weight has the most influence over which resolutions pass. Key mechanics:

  • Resolutions are proposed by members and require a vote to pass or fail.
  • Some resolutions benefit specific ethics (Pacifist resolutions benefit Pacifist empires; Militarist resolutions benefit Militarist empires).
  • The Galactic Custodian role (from Nemesis DLC) grants extraordinary powers to address crisis situations.
  • The Galactic Emperor role transforms the Galactic Community into a direct imperial hierarchy under your rule — the most powerful diplomatic end-state in the game.
The Diplomatic Victory Path

Becoming Galactic Emperor through legitimate Galactic Community mechanics is one of Stellaris's most satisfying victory conditions. The path: accumulate massive Diplomatic Weight (Corporate authority, Trade League federation, Xenophile ethics, high economic output), dominate Community voting, pass the resolutions that increase your Diplomatic Weight further, become Custodian during a Crisis, then convert the Custodianship into Emperor status. This requires Federations and Nemesis DLC and 100+ years of sustained diplomatic work. It is also the most politically interesting path in the game.

§31 Espionage

The Espionage system (Nemesis DLC) allows empires to conduct covert operations against each other using Spy Networks built up over time. Espionage is an intelligence game layered over the main strategy game, with distinct mechanics for offensive operations and defensive counter-intelligence.

Spy Networks

You build a Spy Network in a target empire by assigning Envoys to espionage duty against that target. Network strength increases over time (capped at 100). Higher network strength enables more powerful operations and reduces the chance of exposure. Your own Empire's Encryption level vs. the target's counter-intelligence determines how quickly your network builds and how likely operations are to be detected.

Key Operations

  • Gather Intelligence: Reveals the target empire's fleet composition, research, and economy details.
  • Smear Campaign: Reduces the target's Diplomatic Weight and opinion with third parties.
  • Steal Technology: Chance to acquire a technology the target has but you don't.
  • Arm Dissidents: Triggers internal instability and faction unrest in the target.
  • Crisis Beacon: Causes the target to be designated by the End-Game Crisis as a high-priority target.
  • Become the Crisis: A unique progression mechanic (Nemesis) allowing your empire to become the End-Game Crisis.
Steal Technology — The Highest Value Operation

Steal Technology is the highest-value Espionage operation for most builds. A successful steal gives you a technology that your opponent spent years researching, for the cost of an operation and the risk of detection. Target an empire that is technologically ahead of you and prioritise stealing their most advanced Physics or Engineering technologies. A single successful Steal Technology can advance your research by 10–20 years.

§32 Fallen & Awakened Empires

They had been there before the first hyperlane route was charted. They had been there before the first colonisation ship left the first homeworld. They had been there, in those systems, sleeping — or something so close to sleeping that the distinction was purely philosophical — for longer than the young empires had names for. When they woke, the young empires had two choices: negotiate, or regret. Most chose to negotiate. Some, having insufficient resources for regret, also chose to negotiate. The negotiations were, by most accounts, deeply unpleasant.

// XENODIPLOMACY MANUAL · FALLEN EMPIRE RELATIONS · CHAPTER 1 //

Fallen Empires are ancient civilisations that reached technological heights far beyond current empires and then stagnated into isolation. They are present at game start and dormant — but they will react to perceived threats and provocations. An Awakened Fallen Empire (which happens in response to specific Crisis events or sufficient provocation) is one of the most militarily powerful entities in the game.

Fallen Empire Types

Enigmatic Observers
SPIRITUALIST · MATERIALIST OPPOSITION

Oppose materialist development; will intervene against synthetic life. Treat primitive civilisations as sacred. The galaxy's most pronounced anti-robot Fallen Empire. Avoid uplift events that they oppose if you rely on robotic production.

Militant Isolationists
XENOPHOBE · TERRITORY GUARDIAN

Will not tolerate other empires colonising or claiming systems near their territory. Have well-defined buffer zones. Cross these and face immediate military response. The easiest Fallen Empire to accidentally provoke by expanding in the wrong direction.

Keepers of Knowledge
MATERIALIST · RESEARCH ORIENTED

Value research and science. Can be negotiated with to unlock unique technologies. Will offer tech rewards for specific actions but also react poorly to certain types of primitive civilisation interaction. The most accessible Fallen Empire for trade relationships.

Fallen Empire (War)
MILITARIST · XENOPHOBE · DORMANT CONQUERORS

Former conquering empires that stopped expanding. They remember conquest. Awakening these during the War in Heaven makes them extremely aggressive. The most dangerous to be neighbours with because their memories of empire are violent.

Dealing with Fallen Empires

The standard advice for new players: leave Fallen Empires alone. Do not colonise their buffer systems; do not uplift species they consider sacred; do not build megastructures in their claimed territory. The military cost of a Fallen Empire attack is near-insurmountable in the early-to-mid game. However, once you are technologically advanced (typically year 80+), Awakened Fallen Empires are among the best sources of unique technologies and their remnant buildings — unlocked by the Arcane Deciphering tech — provide unique bonuses unavailable anywhere else.

§33 End-Game Crises

The first contact report from the Observation Post at Kerenthis read: "Unknown fleet, estimated 800 vessels, classification Unbidden, purpose: unclear but directional, moving toward inhabited systems at 0.7c. Advisory: prepare." Fleet Command's response was two words. The first word was a profanity common to seventeen of the galaxy's seventeen known languages. The second word was "acknowledged." The mobilisation began the same hour.

// KERENTHIS OBSERVATION POST · CRISIS ALERT TRANSMISSION · YEAR 2377 //

End-Game Crises are galaxy-threatening events that occur in the late game (typically year 100–150 depending on settings). They are designed to unite the galaxy against a common enemy — or to test whether the galaxy can be united at all. Three Crisis types exist, and additional Crises have been added through updates.

The Three Standard Crises

The Unbidden
EXTRADIMENSIONAL INVADERS · PORTAL-BASED

Arrive through a dimensional portal and spread outward, converting all planets to their own dimension. Pure military challenge: enormous fleet power, hostile to all biological life. The most straightforwardly militarily threatening crisis. Build the largest fleet you can before they arrive. Close the portal if possible; eliminate their bases if you cannot. The Unbidden have been strengthened in 4.3 to better survive their spawn location.

The Prethoryn Scourge
BIOLOGICAL INVADERS · ORGANIC FLEET

An organic fleet of impossibly large proportions fleeing something from beyond the galaxy's edge. They consume planets, converting populations to organic matter. The Scourge are harder to eliminate than the Unbidden because their fleet composition favours organic technology. Chemical or missile weapons are most effective against Prethoryn.

The Contingency
MACHINE INTELLIGENCE · STERILIZATION HUBS

An ancient machine intelligence that seeks to eliminate all biological and synthetic life as a threat to galactic equilibrium. The Contingency is the most strategic Crisis: it activates Sterilization Hubs on inhabited worlds that must be destroyed before they convert all life. Highly effective against disorganised or isolated empires. The most intellectually interesting Crisis to face.

Become the Crisis (Nemesis)

With the Nemesis DLC, a player empire can choose to pursue the Menace path and become the Crisis. Gaining Menace through conquest and destruction allows unlocking the Galactic Nemesis Ascension Perk, which unlocks Colossus planets-killing options and eventually Menacing Ships. It is an alternative win condition that transforms your own empire into the galaxy-threatening force others must unite against. Challenging to sustain but uniquely powerful if executed correctly.

Crisis Preparation

Every game of Stellaris should have Crisis preparation as a mid-game planning consideration:

  • Maintain 2–3 times your normal peacetime fleet in reserve Alloy production capacity. When the Crisis hits, you need to build ships rapidly.
  • Starbases with Anchorages and defensive platforms on your frontiers slow Crisis fleet advances while you build.
  • The Galactic Custodian role (available through the Galactic Community during a Crisis) provides significant bonuses to coordinating a galactic response.
  • Crisis strength in 4.3 scales with difficulty setting. On higher difficulties, prepare earlier and more substantially.

§34 Victory Conditions

Stellaris does not have a single fixed victory condition. The game ends at a target year (default 2500) with a score-based ranking, but players typically define their own victory narratives. The most commonly pursued "win" conditions are:

Victory Paths

Galactic Domination
MILITARY · CONQUEST

Control enough of the galaxy through direct ownership and subject empires to dominate the Galactic Community and win on score. The most direct path. Requires sustained military investment and the administrative capacity to manage a large empire or extensive subject network.

Diplomatic Victory
POLITICAL · GALACTIC EMPEROR

Become Galactic Emperor through legitimate Community mechanics. Requires Federations + Nemesis DLC. The longest and most politically demanding path; also the most rewarding. A Galactic Emperor commands all other empires through institutionalised power rather than military force.

Technological Supremacy
RESEARCH · ASCENSION

Complete an Ascension Path, build Megastructures, achieve the maximum technology level, and end the game with the most advanced civilisation in the galaxy. Not a formal win state but a deeply satisfying completion arc that compounds every other victory condition.

Crisis Victory
NEMESIS DLC · BECOME THE CRISIS

Become the Galactic Nemesis and defeat the galaxy's combined resistance. The most aggressive and unconventional victory path. Requires sustained Menace accumulation and willingness to be at war with the entire galaxy simultaneously. Extremely challenging on higher difficulties.

Survive the Crisis, Then Win

In practical terms, the most important "victory condition" in any Stellaris game is surviving the End-Game Crisis. An empire that survives the Crisis — either alone or as part of a galactic coalition — and emerges with its economy and military intact is in a position to win whatever the scoring condition demands. The Crisis is the true climax of the game's mid-to-late arc. Plan for it, survive it, and the endgame score will follow.

§35 Glossary

Admiral
Leader type that commands fleets; provides combat bonuses based on skill and traits; their Council position provides empire-wide naval bonuses.
Alloys
Strategic resource produced by Foundry jobs; required for ship construction, megastructures, and military buildings; the primary constraint on military expansion.
Amenities
A happiness resource produced by Entertainer and similar jobs; insufficient Amenities reduce Pop Happiness which reduces Stability and economic output.
Ascension Perk
A major empire-defining choice unlocked by completing Tradition Trees; includes Ascension Path gatekeepers, planetary transformations, and powerful passive bonuses.
Authority
The government archetype (Democratic, Oligarchic, Dictatorial, Imperial, Corporate, Hive Mind, Machine Intelligence) determining how power is structured and which civics are available.
Awakened Empire
A Fallen Empire that has been roused from dormancy, typically by specific Crisis events or a War in Heaven; becomes one of the most militarily powerful entities in the galaxy.
Branch Office
MegaCorp DLC facility built on other empires' worlds; generates Trade Value for the Corporate empire without requiring ownership of the planet.
Building Slot
The available spaces on a planet for Buildings; generated primarily by City Districts; the binding constraint on advanced specialist production.
Casus Belli
A specific justification for declaring war; each Casus Belli enables specific War Goals; some are automatic (Claimed Systems), others require specific circumstances.
Civic
A specific institutional principle defining how your empire functions; most empires have two at game start, three after researching Galactic Administration.
Colossus
A unique ship type (Apocalypse DLC) capable of destroying, pacifying, or transforming entire planets; requires specific Ascension Perk; diplomatically provocative to use.
Consumer Goods
Resource produced by Artisan jobs; consumed by all standard Pops as upkeep; deficit causes Happiness collapse; the most common new-player economic crisis.
Contingency
One of three End-Game Crises; an ancient machine intelligence activating Sterilization Hubs on inhabited worlds to eliminate all biological and synthetic life.
Council
Galactic Paragons DLC feature; group of Leaders filling key government positions that provide empire-wide passive bonuses based on their skill and traits.
Diplomatic Weight
The measure of an empire's influence in the Galactic Community; generated by economy, military, technology, and specific bonuses; determines voting power.
District
The basic economic structure on a planet (City, Industrial, Agricultural, Mining, Generator); provides base jobs; can be specialised in 4.x for additional jobs of a specific type.
Drone
The Gestalt equivalent of a Pop job; used by Hive Minds and Machine Intelligences; no Happiness, no Consumer Goods upkeep, unique job types.
Dyson Sphere
Megastructure surrounding an entire star; when complete provides +1000 Energy Credits per month; the highest-value single megastructure in the game.
Ecumenopolis
An entire planet covered in a single city; Ascension Perk transformation; the premier Alloy production platform; capped at one per empire.
Edict
A temporary policy activated for a duration using Influence (or Unity for some types); provides specific production or military bonuses; doubled in duration by the Executive Vigor Ascension Perk.
Enclave
An independent non-empire organisation in the galaxy (Caravaneers, Salvagers, Artisan Troupe, Curators, Mercenaries); offers unique services in exchange for resources or fees.
Empire Sprawl
Administrative complexity measure that increases with your empire's size; generates a penalty to research and tradition costs; offset by Bureaucrat jobs and specific traditions.
Energy Credits
The universal upkeep currency; powers buildings, ships, and pops; generated by Technician jobs and power stations; deficit takes buildings offline.
Envoy
A diplomatic agent with limited availability; assigned to improve or harm relations, operate in the Federation or Galactic Community, or conduct espionage.
Ethics
The fundamental political and philosophical positioning of your empire (Egalitarian/Authoritarian, Materialist/Spiritualist, Pacifist/Militarist, Xenophile/Xenophobe); determines available civics and diplomatic compatibility.
Faction
An internal political movement representing a subset of your population with aligned ethics; generates Influence when happy; demands policy changes; a political management challenge in standard empires.
Fallen Empire
An ancient, technologically superior empire in dormancy at game start; present across multiple systems; reacts to provocation; can Awaken in response to the War in Heaven.
Federation
A multi-empire alliance with shared fleet and governance; multiple types available with different strategic focuses; the President controls the Federation fleet.
Food
Resource produced by Farmer jobs; consumed by standard organic Pops; deficit causes growth penalty and loyalty reduction; Lithoid species eat Minerals instead.
Galactic Community
The galaxy-wide legislative body formed when sufficient empires establish communications; passes resolutions affecting all members; requires Federations DLC for full functionality.
Galactic Custodian
Special role granted by the Galactic Community during a Crisis; provides extraordinary powers; can be converted to Galactic Emperor status with Nemesis DLC.
Gestalt Consciousness
The special ethic that encompasses both Hive Mind and Machine Intelligence; takes all 3 ethics points; creates a fundamentally different playstyle from standard empires.
Governor
Leader type assigned to manage a Sector; provides output and stability bonuses to all planets in the sector; in 4.3, Commander Governors apply Martial Law with direct Stability bonuses.
Habitability
The compatibility percentage between a species and a planet type; in 4.0, affects job efficiency directly (not just happiness); low habitability means less productive workers.
Hive Mind
Gestalt consciousness type; organic collective; no factions; no happiness; Drone jobs; Food upkeep; unique civics including Devouring Swarm and Terravore.
Hyperlane
The only FTL travel method in the standard post-2.0 game; fleets can only travel along established hyperlane connections; creates natural chokepoints and strategic topology.
Influence
The resource of territorial and political power; generated slowly; spent on claiming systems, activating edicts, diplomatic actions, and starbases; almost always the binding constraint on early expansion.
Machine Intelligence
Gestalt consciousness type; synthetic collective; Pop Assembly instead of growth; no Food or Consumer Goods upkeep; unique civics including Driven Assimilator and Rogue Servitor.
Menace
Nemesis DLC resource accumulated by destructive actions when pursuing the Become the Crisis path; unlocks Galactic Nemesis Ascension Perk and Menacing Ships.
Minerals
Basic construction resource produced by Miner jobs; spent on building and district construction and alloy production; deficit halts all construction.
Naval Capacity
The ceiling on fleet size; exceeding it doubles ship upkeep; increased by technology, starbases, traditions, and civics.
Origin
Your empire's backstory and starting conditions; one of the most impactful empire creation choices; affects starting world, resources, relationships, and sometimes win conditions.
Pop Group
The 4.0 replacement for individual Pop tracking; groups pops by species/stratum/job into cohesive units that contribute Workforce to jobs; produces output based on workforce filling job slots.
Prethoryn Scourge
One of three End-Game Crises; an organic biological fleet of enormous scale consuming and converting planets; weak to chemical/missile weapons.
Research Agreement
Bilateral diplomatic agreement providing +10% research output to both empires; excellent early-game diplomatic investment if relations are positive.
Ring World
Megastructure with four massive habitable segments providing enormous district capacity; requires an entire star system; one of the most powerful endgame production platforms.
Science Ship
Non-combat exploration vessel crewed by a Scientist leader; performs surveys, investigates anomalies, and assists with research; essential in the early game exploration phase.
Sector
Administrative grouping of systems and colonies; assigned a Governor whose traits and skill level provide bonuses to all worlds within the sector.
Shroud
Extradimensional space accessible through Psionic Ascension; contains powerful entities that can grant bonuses or disasters; risk-reward space unique to Psionic empires.
Stability
A planet's political health score; low Stability reduces all output from jobs; maintained by Happiness, Governors, Enforcers, and specific policies.
Starbase
The space station controlling a star system; provides Naval Capacity, defensive weapons, and module slots; required to claim systems permanently and project military power.
Subject
An empire in a subordinate relationship to your empire; provides tribute, fleet support, and diplomatic weight; can be integrated into the overlord empire over time.
Tradition
A Unity-purchased advancement within one of several Tradition Trees; completing a full tree grants an Ascension Perk slot; traditions provide production and political bonuses.
Titan
The largest standard warship; provides unique fleet-wide Aura bonuses; equipped with the Titan Lance X-slot weapon; one per fleet; unlocked at high Naval Capacity.
Trade Value
Resource produced by Clerk and Merchant jobs; converts to Energy Credits along trade routes; amplified by Xenophile and Pacifist ethics, Megacorp authority, and Trade League federations.
Trait
A species characteristic purchased at empire creation (or gained through Biological Ascension); provides permanent statistical modifiers to Pops of that species.
Unbidden
One of three End-Game Crises; extradimensional invaders arriving through a dimensional portal; converts all biology to their dimension; purely military threat requiring overwhelming fleet response.
Unity
Resource produced by Clergy, Administrator, and Cultural Specialist jobs; spent on Traditions; the speed of your Tradition tree completion and Ascension Perk acquisition.
Vassal
Standard subject type; provides resource tribute and military participation; cannot declare independent wars; can eventually be integrated into the overlord empire.
War Exhaustion
A meter that increases during wars as fleets are destroyed and planets are bombarded; when both sides reach high exhaustion, peace becomes forced; Pacifist empires have lower War Exhaustion generation.
War in Heaven
A late-game event where two Fallen Empires wake and go to war with each other, demanding all other empires choose sides; can trigger both Fallen Empires to Awaken into galaxy-threatening powers.
Workforce
4.x mechanic replacing individual Pop assignment; Pop Groups contribute Workforce to available job slots; output is calculated based on total Workforce filling each job type.
Zro
Strategic resource required for Psionic Ascension progression; found in Shroud deposits and specific map locations; rare and highly valuable to Spiritualist empires.
